Records held & retention status

The statutory retention schedule. Each record type has a period, the event that starts the clock, the legal basis and a citation. Confirm a policy once your DPO has agreed it.

Where each category of personal data physically lives in the Wren database, with live row counts. This is the "records map" an inspector or the ICO may ask for.

A departing child does not have one retention period — they have four, and an archive that cannot tell them apart can only be kept to the longest. That would mean holding an attendance register for twenty-five years, which is a GDPR problem of our own making. So every archived item carries its class and the date it dies, computed from the right anchor: the leaving date, the permission date, or the child's date of birth.

Destroyed

Deletion is the part everyone skips, and it is the part the ICO asks about. Every artefact the retention sweep destroys keeps its row here, with the reason.

Building an archive and destroying an expired one are scripts, not buttons: scripts/b308-archive-leaver.js and scripts/b308-archive-sweep.js. The sweep reports by default and only deletes with --apply. A deletion you can trigger from a browser tab is a deletion that happens by accident.

Request a secure off-site archive

Archives records that are past their active-need window but still within legal retention. They are encrypted with gpg AES256 on this hardware before leaving the box, shipped to encrypted off-site storage, and a SHA-256 recorded. Nothing is deleted from the live database — hot-DB removal needs explicit sign-off.

Archive log

Parent requests for a copy of their child's data (subject access / portability) or for erasure (right to be forgotten). Approving an access request produces the readable memory package plus a machine-readable export. Approving an erasure request removes personal data while retaining legally-required records (safeguarding, accident/medication, financial, attendance) per the retention schedule. Every step is written to the audit log.
