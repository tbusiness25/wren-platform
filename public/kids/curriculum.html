What each game teaches

Every Wren Kids game starts from a line of the Early Years Foundation Stage framework or the national curriculum, and is designed around that line — not given a curriculum label afterwards.

Which framework. The EYFS games are mapped to the EYFS statutory framework for group and school-based providers, in force 1 September 2026, and to Wren's own Fledge progression statements, which is how a nursery using Wren records what a child can do. The Key Stage games quote the national curriculum programmes of study for England. There is no official machine-readable identifier for a national curriculum statement, so those are given in words.

How these were chosen

We reviewed the best-regarded children's learning apps on the Play Store, the App Store, Amazon Kids+ and the main UK education publishers, read the DfE and WHO screen-use guidance and the Education Endowment Foundation's evidence on digital technology, and then built the eight games that were missing rather than the eight that would be easiest to make. The full research note, with every source and the date it was checked, is kept with the nursery's own documentation.

Three ideas run through all of them: retrieval practice (being asked, and trying to remember, is what makes something stick), spacing (what a child gets wrong comes back sooner), and concrete, pictorial, abstract (in maths the picture always comes before the symbol).
