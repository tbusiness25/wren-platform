🍰 Fraction Forest

Fractions, with the picture before the symbol. Free, no login, no adverts, works offline.

Play Fraction Forest

What it practises

Naming fractions, equivalence, fractions of an amount and comparing.

KS2 Mathematics. Year 3: "recognise and show, using diagrams, equivalent fractions with small denominators". Year 4: equivalent fractions and fractions of a quantity. Year 5: compare and order fractions. Year 6: add and subtract fractions with the same denominator.

Age and stage

Key Stage 2 (Years 3–6) · ages 7–11 · KS2 maths: fractions

Levels

Five: name the shaded fraction, equivalence with bars, equivalence without, fraction of an amount, comparing and adding. The game moves a child up after three right in a row and down after two wrong, so it settles where they are rather than where a year group says they should be.

How it works

Every question shows a divided bar first and the symbol second, and the child moves between them. By level 5 the bar is gone and only the numbers are left.

Why it is built this way

Fractions are where primary maths most often comes apart, and the reason is nearly always that the symbols arrived before the picture. Three-quarters is a number, not two numbers stacked up; 2/4 and 1/2 are the same number; "two thirds of 12" is a sharing action. All three are obvious with a bar in front of you and opaque without one.

Away from the screen

Cut things up and name the parts — half a sandwich, a quarter of an apple. Ask which is more, a third or a quarter, and then draw it.

A round takes two to three minutes, and the app prompts a break after about five. For under-fives, UK guidance is clear that what matters most is that screen time is short and shared with an adult who talks to them about it — so sit with them if you can.
