🔷 Pattern Path

Find the missing stepping stone. Free, no login, no adverts, works offline.

Play Pattern Path

What it practises

Copying, continuing and completing repeating patterns.

EYFS statutory framework, area of learning: Mathematics. Early Learning Goals: ELG12b.

Age and stage

EYFS (under 5s) · ages 3–6 · Early maths and number sense

Levels

Five: AB, ABB/AAB, ABC, AABB with the gap in the middle, and four-element units. The game moves a child up after three right in a row and down after two wrong, so it settles where they are rather than where a year group says they should be.

How it works

A line of stepping stones follows a pattern with one missing. Early levels put the gap at the end; later ones put it in the middle, which cannot be solved by copying the last thing you saw.

Why it is built this way

Spotting and continuing a pattern is an Early Learning Goal in its own right and the root of algebraic thinking — finding the unit of repeat, holding it, and applying it. Most pattern games never go past AB; this one climbs to ABB, ABC and AABB. Every element is a distinct shape as well as a distinct colour, so a colour-blind child plays the same game.

Away from the screen

Lay out cup, spoon, cup, spoon and ask what comes next. Clap-clap-stamp. Thread beads. Say the pattern out loud together.

A round takes two to three minutes, and the app prompts a break after about five. For under-fives, UK guidance is clear that what matters most is that screen time is short and shared with an adult who talks to them about it — so sit with them if you can.
