Play Rhyme Time

What it practises

Hearing rhyme and initial sounds — the oral skills that come before phonics.

EYFS statutory framework, area of learning: Literacy. Early Learning Goals: ELG08c.

Age and stage

EYFS (under 5s) · ages 3–5 · Phonics and reading (the listening that comes first)

Levels

Five: rhyme with 2 pictures, rhyme with 3, odd one out, initial sounds, and mixed odd-one-out of 4. The game moves a child up after three right in a row and down after two wrong, so it settles where they are rather than where a year group says they should be.

How it works

A picture and a spoken word. The child taps the picture that rhymes with it, or later the one that starts with the same sound, or the odd one out. No letters are shown until level 4.

Why it is built this way

This is phonological awareness, not phonics: it is entirely oral, and it is the strongest early predictor of how smoothly systematic synthetic phonics will go later. It deliberately does not teach letters and sounds — Wren Phonics does that properly and for free at phonics.getwren.co.uk, and this game links there.

Away from the screen

Make up silly rhyming pairs at nappy time. "I spy something beginning with /s/" — the sound, not the letter name. Read rhyming books and stop before the rhyme so they fill it in.

A round takes two to three minutes, and the app prompts a break after about five. For under-fives, UK guidance is clear that what matters most is that screen time is short and shared with an adult who talks to them about it — so sit with them if you can.
