🔎 Spell Spotter

Spot the right spelling — and learn the rule. Free, no login, no adverts, works offline.

Play Spell Spotter

What it practises

Year 1 and Year 2 statutory spelling — common exception words and the named rules.

KS1 English, Appendix 1 (Spelling): the Year 1 and Year 2 common exception words; -tch, -dge and -ck after a short vowel; adding -ing, -ed, -er and -est; plurals with -s and -es; contractions.

Age and stage

Key Stage 1 (Years 1–2) · ages 5–7 · KS1 English: spelling and SPaG

Levels

Five: Year 1 common exception words, Year 2 common exception words, -tch/-dge/-ck, adding -ing and -ed, plurals and contractions. The game moves a child up after three right in a row and down after two wrong, so it settles where they are rather than where a year group says they should be.

How it works

A real sentence with one word missing, read aloud, and three spellings to choose from. Get it wrong and you are told the rule, not just that you were wrong.

Why it is built this way

Every wrong spelling offered is an error children actually make — writen, becuase, cach, hopping-for-hoping. A randomly mangled word teaches nothing, because no child would ever have written it. And the rule is the part that transfers: "catch needs -tch after a short vowel" is useful next week; "wrong" is not.

Away from the screen

Ask them to tell you the rule back. Spot the pattern in a book — how many -tch words can you find on this page?

A round takes two to three minutes, and the app prompts a break after about five. For under-fives, UK guidance is clear that what matters most is that screen time is short and shared with an adult who talks to them about it — so sit with them if you can.
