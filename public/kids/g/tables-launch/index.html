Play Tables Launch

What it practises

Times tables to 12 × 12, weighted like the statutory Year 4 check.

KS2 Mathematics. Year 4: "recall multiplication and division facts for multiplication tables up to 12 × 12". Year 2: the 2, 5 and 10 tables. Year 3: the 3, 4 and 8 tables. Rehearses the format of the statutory Multiplication Tables Check.

Age and stage

Key Stage 2 (Years 3–6) · ages 7–11 · KS2 maths: times tables

Levels

Five: the 2s, 5s and 10s; plus 3s, 4s and 8s; all tables to 12; division facts; Check mode. The game moves a child up after three right in a row and down after two wrong, so it settles where they are rather than where a year group says they should be.

How it works

Multiplication and division facts, four answers to choose from. Level 5 is Check mode: typed on a keypad, six seconds a question, like the real thing.

Why it is built this way

Most tables apps drill 1×1 to 12×12 evenly, which spends most of a child's practice on facts they already know. The statutory check does not: it draws from the 2 to 12 tables (the 1× table is excluded) and emphasises the 6, 7, 8, 9 and 12 tables because those are the hardest. This game weights its questions the same way, keeps a per-fact record on the device so wrong facts return sooner, and has no timer at all on levels 1 to 4.

This is practice in the official format, not an official practice tool. The Standards and Testing Agency's own try-it-out area sits behind a school sign-in.

Away from the screen

Short and often. Pick one table a week and ask it out of order, including the division facts — 42 ÷ 6 is the same knowledge as 6 × 7.

A round takes two to three minutes, and the app prompts a break after about five. For under-fives, UK guidance is clear that what matters most is that screen time is short and shared with an adult who talks to them about it — so sit with them if you can.
